POSTSCRIPT CONCERNING THE ENCRAVINGS.

With respect to the Engravings, no pains have been spared that they should be properly executed, and that the drawings should be correct. Thc distant prospect of Galway, and the views of the church, Lynch's castle, the county court-house, bridge, and prison, were taken by an ingenious artist, blought from Dublin solely for the purpose. The armorial bearings, contained in plate II, have been extracted partly from the old map of the town. This latter document, which the author, after much trouble, has been enabled to lay before the reader, affords incontestible proof of the former respectable state of Galway, and is deservedly admired as one of the principal topographical curiosities relating to Ireland. [[1]] A drawing of Lynch's castle, situate in the centre of the town, is given, as it presents a beautiful specimen of ancient architecture, and had formerly been the residence of some of the chief magistrates. The modern plan of the town and suburbs has been made from actual survey, and will, it is hoped, be found generally correct. The miscellaneous plate also contains an assemblage possessing some local interest, and, although the different objects are reduced to a small scale, yet care has been taken that they should all be exactly delineated. The principal engravings have been executed by some of the best engravers which Dublin could afford.

The only copy, whieh until lately, was known to be extant in Ireland, was preserved at Castlemagarrett, in the county Mayo, and the proprietor, very properly, esteeming it as an unique of eonsiderable value, appeared disinelined to hazard its safety by transmitting it to Dublin. An ingenious surveyor of that eounty deelared that he eould not furnish a eopy of it for less than 1001. In the meantime a duplieate was discovered in the valuable library of Trinity College. Here the author met with no diffieulty; his artist was allowed to take a faesimile, which, omitting some of the etxra ornaments, has been redueed, and engraved on as large a seale as possible for this work: and thus he has been enabled, though at some expense, to indulge his own wish, and gratify the publie euriosity, by introdueing (and perhaps reseuing from oblivion) this interesting relie of former times.
